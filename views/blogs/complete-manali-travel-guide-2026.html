Manali is more than a hill station — it is Himachal Pradesh's crown jewel, a high-altitude retreat that blends adventure with ancient culture, alpine meadows with roaring rivers, and cosy cafés with dramatic snowfields. Sitting at 2,050 metres (6,726 ft) above sea level in the Kullu Valley, this small town has quietly become one of India's most-visited destinations.

In 2026, Manali is better connected than ever. The Atal Tunnel has slashed travel time to Lahaul-Spiti, new boutique hotels have opened along the Beas River, and the government has introduced advance permit booking for Rohtang Pass. This guide covers every detail you need for a seamless, memorable trip.

Best Time to Visit Manali

Manali is a year-round destination, but each season offers a dramatically different experience. Choosing the right time can make or break your trip.

🌸 March – June (Peak Season)

This is Manali's most popular window. Temperatures hover between 10°C and 25°C, the Beas River runs full from snowmelt, and Rohtang Pass typically opens in May. Solang Valley is alive with paragliders and zorbing. Expect crowds and book accommodation at least 3 weeks in advance.

💡 Pro Tip

Visit in April or early May to enjoy spring blooms and snow on the peaks without the peak-June crowd. Apple orchards in Naggar look spectacular at this time.

☁️ July – September (Monsoon)

Rainfall is heavy, and landslides frequently block the Manali–Leh and Manali–Chandigarh highways. That said, the valley turns impossibly lush and green. If you're flexible and adventurous, this can be a magical — if risky — time to visit. Travel insurance is a must.

⚠️ Landslide Warning

Always check the HP Road Status portal (hppolice.gov.in) before driving during monsoon. Keep an emergency buffer of 2–3 extra days in your itinerary.

🍁 October – November (Autumn)

The weather is crisp, the skies are crystal clear, and the crowds have thinned. Apple harvest is in full swing. Temperatures drop sharply after mid-November — an early sign of the winter ahead. This is one of the best times for photography and trekking to Kheerganga.

❄️ December – February (Winter)

Manali transforms into a snow globe. The town centre usually gets 2–4 feet of snow, while Solang Valley becomes a skiing hub. Hotels slash rates by up to 50%. Pack extreme-cold-weather gear — temperatures can drop to –10°C or below.

"Manali in October is the best-kept secret in Indian tourism. The air is sharp, the apples are ripe, and the mountains look like they've been freshly painted."
— Deepak Chauhan, Travel Writer

How to Reach Manali

✈️ By Air

The nearest airport is Bhuntar (Kullu-Manali Airport), approximately 50 km from Manali town. IndiGo, Air India, and SpiceJet operate daily flights from Delhi and occasionally from Mumbai. A taxi from the airport to Manali costs ₹900–1,400.

🚌 By Bus

HRTC (Himachal Road Transport Corporation) runs overnight Volvo and ordinary buses from Delhi's Kashmere Gate ISBT. The journey takes 13–16 hours depending on traffic. Tickets start at ₹700 for ordinary and go up to ₹1,600 for Volvo AC. Private operators like RedBus and AbhiBus offer 3×2 sleeper coaches.

💡 Bus Tip

Book HRTC Volvo tickets at least 2 weeks ahead for June and October. Window seats on the left side (Delhi → Manali) give you the best Beas River views as you enter Kullu Valley.

🚗 By Road (Self-Drive)

From Delhi, take NH44 to Ambala, then NH5 (old NH22) through Chandigarh → Shimla → Kullu → Manali. The scenic route via Shimla is longer (~560 km) but breathtaking. An alternate faster route goes via Chandigarh → Bilaspur → Mandi → Manali (~540 km). Both take 13–16 hours.

🚆 By Train + Bus

No direct train to Manali exists. The closest major railheads are Chandigarh (310 km) and Ambala (360 km). From either station, catch a Volvo bus or hire a taxi for the onward journey.

Top Places to Visit in Manali

Manali punches well above its weight in terms of attractions. Here are the must-visit spots in and around town.

1. Rohtang Pass (3,978 m)

The crown jewel of any Manali trip, Rohtang offers jaw-dropping views of glaciers and the Chandra River Valley. In 2026, the HP Tourism authority requires an online permit booked 24 hours in advance. Only 1,200 vehicles are permitted per day. Snow activities like sledging and snow scooters are available. Read our complete Rohtang Pass guide →

2. Solang Valley

Just 14 km north of Manali, Solang is the adventure capital of Himachal. In summer: paragliding, zorbing, ATV rides, and zip-lining. In winter: skiing, snowboarding, and snow scooters. The cable car (ropeway) offers aerial views of the valley for ₹500 return.

3. Hadimba Temple

Built in 1553 AD, this multi-tiered pagoda-style temple dedicated to Goddess Hadimba (Hidimba) sits inside a cedar forest. The temple is architecturally unique — constructed without mortar — and is considered sacred by the Kullu royal family. Entry is free; photography of the idol is prohibited.

4. Vashisht Village & Hot Springs

A 3 km drive from Manali, Vashisht village is home to natural sulphur hot springs inside an ancient bathhouse. The waters are said to have therapeutic properties. The area is also popular with backpackers for its cafés and relaxed vibe.

5. Old Manali

Cross the Manalsu stream to find the original settlement — a tangle of narrow lanes, apple orchards, boutique guesthouses, and bohemian cafés. The 500-year-old Manu Maharishi Temple here is the starting point of many travellers' spiritual journeys in the valley.

💡 Local Secret

Skip the overpriced tourist cafés on the main road in Old Manali. Instead, walk up to "Lazy Dog" or "Drifters Inn" tucked in the orchards — better food, better views, half the price.

Things to Do in Manali

Whether you're an adrenaline junkie or a slow traveller, Manali has something for everyone.

  • Paragliding — Solang Valley and Dober Hill offer tandem flights with certified instructors (₹1,500–3,500).
  • White-Water Rafting — Grade 2–4 rapids on the Beas River between Pirdi and Jhiri (₹600–1,200 per person).
  • Trekking — Hampta Pass (3 days, Grade: Moderate), Chandrakhani Pass (4 days), Beas Kund (2 days, easy).
  • Skiing & Snowboarding — Solang Valley has a ski slope with equipment rental available Nov–March.
  • Mountain Biking — Hire a bike in Old Manali and ride the Leh Highway toward Rohtang.
  • Camping — Riverside camps on the Beas near Katrain or forest camps near Sethan Village (₹700–2,000 per night with meals).
  • Heritage Walk — Naggar Castle, the Nicholas Roerich Art Gallery, and the Tripura Sundari Temple make for a cultural half-day.
⚠️ Altitude Caution

If you're arriving from a low-altitude city, spend your first day at rest and stay well-hydrated. Avoid strenuous activities for 24 hours. Symptoms of AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness) include headache, nausea, and dizziness — descend immediately if they persist.
